# OpenTelemetry vs. Living AI Knowledge — Adversarial Comparison

**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Verified against primary sources:** semantic-conventions `main` (entity YAML pulled directly from `raw.githubusercontent.com`, 2026-09-24), specification `entities/data-model.md` and `entities/entity-events.md` (both status **Development**), `opentelemetry-proto` `common/v1/common.proto` + `resource/v1/resource.proto` at `main`, `weaver_mcp/README.md`, `collector-contrib/connector/servicegraphconnector/README.md`, the published semconv registry pages for `code` and `entities`, and the OTel blog post *"What can you do with OpenTelemetry entity events?"* (2026).

---

## 0. Verification: where the dossier is right, and where it is wrong

The prior dossier is substantially accurate. I confirmed verbatim, from source:

- `specification/entities/data-model.md`, status **Development**: *"Entity relationship modelling will be refined in future specification work."*
- `entity-events.md`: *"Resource data cannot contain relationship information."* and *"Relationship types form an open enumeration. Standard relationship types SHOULD be defined in OpenTelemetry semantic conventions."*
- I grepped every `model/**/entities.yaml` in the semconv repo for the string `relationship`. **Zero hits.** No standard relationship type is defined anywhere in the semconv model. The edge vocabulary is genuinely, literally empty.
- `EntityRef` in `common.proto` is exactly `{schema_url, type, id_keys[], description_keys[]}` — key references into `resource.attributes`, no values, no edges. `Resource.entity_refs = 3` is annotated `Status: [Development]`, `[Since v1.6.0]`.
- The `code` namespace has **exactly five** non-deprecated attributes: `code.column.number`, `code.file.path`, `code.function.name`, `code.line.number`, `code.stacktrace`.
- `entity.vcs.repository` identity is `vcs.repository.url.full` (required, identifying); `vcs.ref` identity is `vcs.ref.head.revision`. Neither has any relationship to a `service`.
- All eight `weaver registry mcp` tools are read-only. Nothing in first-party OTel accepts a fact write.
- servicegraphconnector: alpha, 2s store TTL, 1000-item cap, `db.name` collapse, six Prometheus series, and the hard requirement that *"If spans of a trace are spread out over multiple instances, spans are not paired up reliably."*

**Three corrections, all of which make OTel look weaker than the dossier implies:**

**(1) The infrastructure entity vocabulary is not stable — it is overwhelmingly Development.** The dossier calls infra modelling "arguably best-in-class" and leans on CNCF graduation for stability. I counted stability across every entity group in `model/`: **5 stable, 5 release_candidate, 50 development.** The five stable entity types are `service`, `service.instance`, `service.namespace`, `telemetry.sdk`, `telemetry.distro`. The five release candidates are `cicd.pipeline`, `cicd.pipeline.run`, `cicd.worker`, `process`, `vcs.repository`. **Every single Kubernetes, host, container, cloud, and FaaS entity type is Development status.** The individual *attributes* (`k8s.pod.uid`, `container.id`) are stable; the entity *groupings* that turn those attributes into an identity contract are not. Anyone who adopts OTel entity identities for k8s today is adopting an unstable spec.

**(2) OTel's entity identity model actively fails the env-discrimination problem the concept names as a core requirement.** `entity.service` identity is `service.name` **alone** (required, identifying). `service.version` and `service.criticality` are descriptive. Environment lives on a *separate* `deployment` entity — and here is the sharp bit: `entity.deployment` declares `deployment.environment.name` with **no `role:` tag at all**, so it is neither identifying nor descriptive, and the `deployment` entity therefore has **zero declared identifying attributes.** Combine that with the merge rule — *"Entities MAY be merged if and only if their types are the same, their identity attributes are exactly the same AND their schema_url is the same"* — and the consequence is that a prod `checkout-service` and a staging `checkout-service` have **identical OTel entity identity and are merge-eligible by spec.** The Living AI Knowledge concept treats `env` as a first-class discriminator on the entity precisely so `prod-redis` and `staging-redis` never merge. OTel gets this wrong by construction. This is a real, concrete, verified point in the concept's favour and the dossier missed it.

**(3) Entity count.** The dossier says 64; the published registry page reports 80 across 23 namespaces; my direct count of `- id: entity.*` groups in the entity YAMLs came to 60. The number is soft and version-dependent. Not load-bearing, but don't quote "64" as a fact.

One thing the dossier under-credits: entity events are no longer purely theoretical paper. The spec shipped in release v1.58.0 (2026-06-22) and OTel published a 2026 blog post on consuming them. However — and this matters — that post names **no** Collector component that emits `entity.relationships`, and **no** backend that ingests them. The researcher's uncertainty stands: the edge mechanism exists on paper, in Development status, with an empty type vocabulary and no shipped producer or consumer.

There is no object representing "a claim" and no object representing "someone claiming it." | None | **Ours, uncontested.** OTel's entire model assumes the emitter *is* the truth. There is no seam at which a second opinion could attach. |
| **Proposition-as-subject** (claims about claims) | Nothing. `additionalProperties: false` on the Weaver `Entity` schema; no reification anywhere. | None | Ours. OTel's nearest move is nesting attributes under a namespace, which is string-prefixing, not reification. |

A model that forbids uncertain emission would make our write path reject most of what agents actually know. |

## 2. The motivating query, walked through concretely

> *"Where does the list of notifications in Client A come from?"* — Client A → Service C → Redis E → falls back to → Postgres → provisioned by → Terraform.

**Hop 1, Client A → Service C.** OTel can genuinely do this, at runtime, and it is the best thing it does. Both services instrumented, spans flowing to a Collector, `servicegraphconnector` pairs the CLIENT span with the SERVER span on `(traceID, parentSpanID)`, and emits `traces_service_graph_request_total{client="client-a", server="service-c"}`. **Required to configure:** instrumentation in both services, a Collector, a `loadbalancingexporter` layer in front so all spans of a trace land on one connector instance (the README is explicit that without this "spans are not paired up reliably"), a Prometheus, and Grafana ≥9.0.4 with a Tempo datasource to render it. **What you get:** a counter label pair. Not a traversable edge. Not a code location. No evidence pointer. If Client A's notification call is on a code path that didn't execute in the last flush window, the edge does not exist.

**Hop 2, Service C → Redis E.** Also real. A CLIENT span with `db.system.name=redis` is collapsed immediately — the connector does not wait for a server span — into `ServerService = dbName`, `connection_type = database`. **Where it breaks:** the default `virtual_node_peer_attributes` are `[peer.service, db.name, db.system]`. `server.address` is **not** in that list. So prod-redis and staging-redis collapse to the same node label `redis` unless you explicitly configure `dimensions`. This is our named entity-resolution failure mode — over-merge poisoning answers silently — manifesting as a shipped default in an alpha component.

**Hop 3, "falls back to" Postgres.** This is where the whole approach breaks, and not for a fixable reason. A fallback is a *conditional* relation: a property of the code, not of any particular execution. OTel can only see it if the fallback *actually fired* during the observation window. If the cache hit rate is 99.9%, the edge is absent from the service graph 99.9% of the time and its 2-second store TTL guarantees nothing accumulates. Worse: even when it fires, OTel has no way to *label* it as a fallback. There is no `relationship.type` available on a span, span `Link` is `{trace_id, span_id, trace_state, attributes, flags}` with no link-type semantic convention at all, and `SpanKind` distinguishes only CLIENT/SERVER/PRODUCER/CONSUMER/INTERNAL. The Postgres read is indistinguishable from any other Postgres read. **The semantics of the relation are structurally unrecoverable.** A single agent reading `cache.get(key) ?? db.query(...)` in one file gets this for free.

**Hop 4, Postgres → Terraform.** Total blank. There is no Terraform entity, no IaC namespace, no state-file, module, plan, or resource-address concept anywhere in semconv. `cloud.resource_id` (an ARN) exists as a *descriptive* attribute on the Development-status `cloud` entity, so an ARN can ride along on a resource — but nothing maps an ARN to a Terraform address, and even if it did, there is no edge type to express the mapping. You hand-author 100% of this.

**The four repos.** `vcs.repository` exists (RC) with identity `vcs.repository.url.full`. Nothing connects it to a service. To assert `service-c is_defined_in repo-3` you would: define a custom semconv registry in Weaver; invent a relationship type (the enum is open, so this is permitted and entirely non-standard); emit an `entity.state` log record with an `entity.relationships` array; and then — discover there is no first-party Collector component that emits entity relationships and no backend that ingests them. You would write the producer and the consumer and the store. At which point you have rebuilt our schema inside OTel's event envelope, with none of assertion, polarity, provenance, trust, or bitemporality, and you still have nowhere to put it.

**The follow-up, "how do I connect to that database?"** `server.address`, `server.port`, `db.namespace` exist as span attributes — so the connection *coordinates* are recoverable, by querying a trace backend (Tempo/Jaeger/Datadog), not OTel. The *credential location* has no vocabulary at all. And the honest failure mode is the one our own risk register names: `db.query.text` is captured verbatim by many instrumentations and the docs warn it "might contain sensitive data" — OTel's pipelines leak secrets by default in exactly the direction our concept worries about.

**Net:** OTel + a full observability stack can, with meaningful configuration effort, produce hops 1 and 2 as *aggregate metric labels*. It cannot produce hop 3's semantics, hop 4 at all, or the repo dimension at all. And the output is a Grafana node-graph picture, not a cited path with per-edge provenance that an LLM can narrate.

## 3. Threat assessment

**Threat level: low, structurally.** OpenTelemetry is not a company and ships no product. It has no storage layer, no query API, and no revenue motive. Its governance model — spec proposals, SIG consensus, multi-quarter stability ladders — makes shipping a persistent, agent-writable, refutation-capable knowledge store essentially inconceivable. The relationship model has been openly deferred (*"will be refined in future specification work"*) and, as verified, has produced exactly zero standard relationship types.

**What they would have to build to be a competitor:** a storage layer, a query language, multi-hop traversal, an assertion/provenance model, a confidence model, a contradiction model, bitemporality, entity resolution with fuzzy candidates and merge records, a code-structure vocabulary from scratch, and a write API that accepts uncertain input. That is not an extension of OTel; it is a different project that reuses OTel's noun list. OTel's current design choices actively *contradict* several of those (MUST-NOT-emit-when-uncertain; last-write-wins merge; no provenance by design).

**Where the real threat lives:** not in OTel, but in a *vendor* that ingests entity events and already has a topology store, a query API, and revenue — Datadog, Grafana, Dash0, ServiceNow, Elastic. OTel is handing those vendors a standardized entity + relationship wire format. If one of them ships `entity.relationships` ingestion plus a graph query API plus an MCP write endpoint, that is a credible competitor. **The thing to monitor is not the OTel spec repo; it is vendor changelogs for entity-event ingestion.** Two concrete tripwires: (a) a Collector component in contrib that emits populated `entity.relationships`; (b) any standard relationship type landing in `model/` — grep for it quarterly, it is a one-line check.

**Incentive check:** OTel's constituency is observability vendors who want telemetry portability. None of them benefit from OTel shipping a queryable store — that is the layer they sell. The project is structurally disincentivized from closing this gap.

## 4. What to steal — concretely

**1. `role: identifying` vs `role: descriptive`, including the exact naming.** This is independent convergent evidence that our identifying/descriptive qualifier split is the right cut. Steal the *vocabulary*, not the semantics: their descriptive attributes are last-write-wins scalars; ours become propositions-about-propositions that version and can be refuted. Ours is strictly more expressive. But adopt their words so anyone from the observability world reads our schema without translation.

**2. `entity.report.interval` as a third staleness mechanism.** Add a heartbeat contract alongside trust decay and scope sweeps: an importer declares `reassert_interval_seconds` on a scope; the reader can then expire that scope's assertions when the interval lapses *without waiting for a sweep to complete*. This covers the case our design misses — a scan run that dies halfway and never reaps. Steal the explicit rules too: deletes are never guaranteed, may arrive out of order, and a state event arriving *after* a delete must be applied anyway because each state event is the complete current state.

**3. The churn heuristic for edge ownership.** *Put the relationship on the entity with the shorter lifespan / higher churn* (pod→replicaset, not replicaset→pod). We need a rule for which side of an edge owns re-assertion and sweeping; this is a good, cheap one.

**4. Ownership-by-specificity for qualifier placement.** *"MUST be referenced by the most specific entity, the one closest in the topology graph"* — `cloud.availability_zone` belongs to `k8s.node`, not `k8s.cluster`. Direct analogue for descriptive qualifiers: attach to the most specific proposition that can carry it. Prevents a class of duplicate-fact bloat.

**5. Adopt their identifier vocabulary verbatim for strong identifiers.** `k8s.pod.uid`, `k8s.node.uid`, `container.id`, `host.id`, `cloud.resource_id`, `service.name`+`service.namespace`+`service.instance.id`, `vcs.repository.url.full`, `vcs.ref.head.revision`, and the closed enums `db.system.name` (41 values) and `cloud.platform` (~50). Re-deriving these is waste, and matching them makes OTel-import a field rename rather than a mapping layer. **Do not** adopt their entity *identity contracts* wholesale — see the env failure below.

**6. Telemetry Schema URLs — the one thing we have no answer to.** Immutable, permanently-cacheable, versioned vocabulary files at `http[s]://server/path/<version>`, with declared migration transformations and `weaver registry diff`. Our predicate vocabulary *will* drift; `reads_from` will split into `reads_from` / `caches_in`. Right now an assertion written in January and one written in December are silently assumed to mean the same thing. Concrete action: **put a `vocab_version` on the assertion row** (not the proposition), and keep a migration table mapping old predicate → new predicate per version bump. Cheap now, impossible to retrofit later.

**7. `live_check` as the shape of our validation response.** It validates submitted samples against the registry and *returns findings while persisting and rejecting nothing.* That is precisely what our accept-then-normalize write path needs: `POST /v1/observations` still returns 202, but the response body carries `findings: [{severity, path, message}]` — unknown predicate, entity kind mismatch, enum violation, deprecated predicate. The agent gets to self-correct without ever being blocked. Also steal the `output: full | findings_only` switch.

**8. `entity_associations` → predicate domain/range typing.** OTel declares which entity types a metric may attach to (500+ definitions carry this). Our `predicate` table should declare `subject_kinds[]` / `object_kinds[]`. Two payoffs: it powers the findings above, and it lets path templates be *derived* from the predicate table rather than hand-written.

**9. The `OTEL_ENTITIES` propagation grammar** — `type{id=v,...}[desc=v,...]@schema_url`, semicolon-separated, consumed by a required `EnvEntityDetector`. Steal the pattern for CI: an env var that tells a scanning agent what entity it *is* and what scope_key it is writing under, so the agent doesn't have to infer its own identity. A `LAK_SCOPE` env var read at agent startup is a five-line feature that materially improves scope-sweep correctness.

**10. Their `MUST NOT emit` rule — as a warning, not a design.** It is the correct instinct (bad identity is worse than no identity) applied to the wrong emitter class. Our version: never reject, but *never auto-merge on a weak alias either*, and surface low-confidence resolutions in the findings array so the agent can supply a strong identifier next time.

**11. The argument against ourselves, stolen for free.** OTel's identity rules are an explicit bet that *making disagreement impossible beats recording it.* That bet is only wrong because our observers are non-deterministic. Write that down as the falsifiable premise of the assertion model: if our facts turn out to be overwhelmingly importer-derived and deterministic, the proposition/assertion split is expensive machinery for a problem we don't have — which is exactly risk #1 in our own register, arriving from an unexpected direction.

## 5. Build-on vs. compete vs. data source

**Build on it: no, and the reason is structural, not incidental.** The two things a code-plus-infrastructure knowledge graph needs most are *typed edges* and *code structure*. Those are precisely the two things OTel does not define — one deferred by the spec in writing, the other reduced to five string attributes. You cannot build a traversal store on OTLP because OTLP is a wire format with no receiver contract about persistence, and the only graph-shaped output in the entire project is six Prometheus metric families, which cannot be traversed. Building "on" OTel means: adopting its vocabulary (yes, do this), and building everything else (storage, edges, assertions, trust, query) yourself — which is the plan already.

**Compete: not really.** There is no overlapping product surface. We would only collide if a vendor productizes entity events, and that is the vendor's threat, not OTel's.

**Data source: emphatically yes, and this is the right frame — but be honest about what it feeds.**

Three distinct ingestion paths, in descending order of value:

**(a) Entity identity import (highest value, lowest effort).** Run a Collector with `k8sattributesprocessor` + `resourcedetectionprocessor` and consume the Resource attributes. Every span arrives pre-decorated with `service.name`, `service.namespace`, `service.instance.id`, `k8s.pod.uid`, `k8s.namespace.name`, `container.id`, `host.id`, `cloud.provider`, `cloud.region`, `cloud.resource_id`. These are **strong identifiers, machine-generated, and already unique** — exactly what our strong-identifier tables want, and exactly what agents are worst at producing. This single import path is the most direct mitigation available for risk #2 (entity resolution). Method for these assertions: `telemetry`. Trust: high base, high freshness.

**(b) Observed service-dependency edges (high value, medium effort).** Do **not** consume `servicegraphconnector`'s Prometheus output — aggregated labels lose the identity you need. Consume the **spans directly** via an OTLP receiver and do the CLIENT/SERVER pairing yourself, keeping `server.address`, `server.port`, `db.system.name`, `db.namespace`, `messaging.system`, `messaging.destination.name`, `http.route`. Our pairing can be far better than theirs because we have durable storage: their 2-second TTL and 1000-item cap exist only because they hold state in memory. We can pair across minutes, and we can *accumulate* rare edges — which is exactly what makes the fallback-to-Postgres edge observable: it fires rarely, so you need a store that remembers, which is the thing OTel does not have. **This is the single best argument that telemetry import complements rather than duplicates agent assertions.**

**(c) Runtime corroboration and refutation (the strategically interesting one).** A telemetry-derived edge is an *independent observer* of a claim an agent made from source. Agent says `service-c reads_from redis-e` (method `llm_inferred`, evidence: a file and line). The OTLP importer independently asserts the same proposition (method `telemetry`, evidence: a trace id). Same fingerprint, two assertions, corroboration count 2 from two different methods. Conversely, an edge that traffic *stopped* traversing after a deploy is a refutation candidate. **This makes telemetry the cheapest available ground truth for validating the trust score** — which our own register calls out as possibly theater. The AUC experiment (trust vs. hand-labelled truth) gets dramatically cheaper if telemetry supplies a large slice of the labels for free.

**The honest counter-argument, which is risk #1 arriving in force:** an OTLP importer, once built, will out-produce LLM agents by orders of magnitude on edge volume. Telemetry emits continuously; agents emit per-session. If the product's value ends up concentrated in (a)+(b), then the accurate description of the product is "an observability-derived topology store with agent annotations," and the proposition/assertion machinery is expensive scaffolding around an importer. **Concrete mitigation: instrument the fact origin from day one.** Count distinct propositions by `method`, and specifically count propositions whose subject and object resolve to entities from *different repos* and that *only* an agent asserted. If that number stays near zero after a month of real use, the cross-repo thesis is not holding and the product should be rescoped before more months go in.

## 6. Verdict

OpenTelemetry overlaps the Living AI Knowledge concept in roughly one area out of six, and in that area — a typed, vendor-neutral vocabulary for identifying running infrastructure — it is better than anything we would build ourselves and should be adopted rather than re-derived. In every other dimension it has nothing, and in two of them (entity identity merge without an environment discriminator; a prohibition on emitting uncertain identity) its design is actively incompatible with an LLM-written store.

It is not a competitor. It has no storage, no query API, no read-back, no code model, no edges, no provenance, no confidence, and no refutation, and its governance makes acquiring them implausible. It is the best available **data source** for the infrastructure half of the graph and for the runtime corroboration of agent claims — and consuming it well is the most direct available mitigation for the entity-resolution risk and the trust-score-is-theater risk simultaneously.

The thing to actually worry about is downstream: a vendor with an existing topology store shipping `entity.relationships` ingestion plus a graph query API. Set a quarterly tripwire on `grep -ri relationship model/` in the semconv repo and on Collector-contrib components that emit populated entity relationships. Today both return nothing.
